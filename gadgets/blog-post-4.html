Complete Guide to Mechanical Keyboards for Beginners

February 18, 2025 JOMKAKI Team 9 min read

Mechanical keyboards have exploded in popularity, and for good reason. Once you type on a mechanical keyboard, going back to a membrane keyboard feels like a downgrade. But with so many choices in switches, layouts, and brands, getting started can be confusing. This beginner-friendly guide covers everything you need to know.

Why Mechanical Keyboards?

Before diving into the details, here's why people switch to mechanical:

  • Better typing feel: Each keypress has satisfying tactile feedback
  • Durability: Rated for 50-100 million keypresses vs 5 million for membrane
  • Customizability: Swap switches, keycaps, and even the case
  • Faster response: Lower latency for gaming and faster typing
  • Less fatigue: Proper actuation reduces finger strain during long sessions

Understanding Switch Types

The switch is the heart of a mechanical keyboard. There are three main categories:

Linear Switches (Red, Black, Yellow)

Smooth keystroke with no bump or click. The keypress feels consistent from top to bottom. Favoured by gamers for fast, uninterrupted key presses.

  • Feel: Smooth, no tactile bump
  • Sound: Quiet (thock sound)
  • Best for: Gaming, quiet offices
  • Popular: Cherry MX Red, Gateron Yellow, Akko Cream Yellow

Tactile Switches (Brown, Clear)

A noticeable bump at the actuation point tells you exactly when the keypress registers. Great middle ground between typing feedback and noise level.

  • Feel: Bump at actuation point
  • Sound: Moderate
  • Best for: Typing, programming, general use
  • Popular: Cherry MX Brown, Gateron Brown, Holy Panda

Clicky Switches (Blue, Green)

Tactile bump plus an audible click sound. The most satisfying to type on but also the loudest. Not recommended for shared offices.

  • Feel: Bump + audible click
  • Sound: Loud, clicky
  • Best for: Home use, writers who love sound feedback
  • Popular: Cherry MX Blue, Kailh Box White, Gateron Blue

Keyboard Layouts & Sizes

Mechanical keyboards come in various sizes. Here's what each offers:

  • Full Size (100%): Complete with number pad. Best for data entry and accounting
  • Tenkeyless / TKL (80%): Drops the numpad. More desk space, still has function row and arrow keys
  • 75%: Compact layout keeping function row and arrow keys. Very popular for its space efficiency
  • 65%: Drops the function row but keeps arrow keys. Sweet spot for many users
  • 60%: Ultra compact — no function row, no arrow keys. Functions accessed via key layers

Budget Recommendations for Malaysia

You don't need to spend a fortune to get a great mechanical keyboard. Here are our picks by budget:

Under RM150

Royal Kludge RK61 or Redragon K552 — solid entry points with hot-swappable switches and RGB lighting. Perfect for trying out mechanical keyboards without a big commitment.

RM150 - RM300

Keychron C1/C3 Pro or Akko 3068B — better build quality, gasket-mounted designs for better acoustics, and Bluetooth connectivity. The sweet spot for most buyers.

RM300 - RM500

Keychron Q series or NuPhy Air75 V2 — aluminium construction, premium switches, and exceptional typing experience. Worth the investment if you type daily for work.

"A mechanical keyboard isn't just a peripheral — it's a daily companion that makes every keystroke enjoyable. Start with your switch preference and budget, and the rest will follow."

Getting Started Tips

  • Start with tactile (brown) switches if you're unsure — they're the most versatile
  • Get a hot-swappable board so you can try different switches without soldering
  • A TKL or 75% layout works for most people
  • Invest in a good desk mat — it improves sound and protects your desk
  • Watch typing tests on YouTube to hear different switch sounds before buying
