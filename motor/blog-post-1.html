5 Signs Your Motorcycle Absorber Needs Replacement

January 15, 2025 JOMKAKI Team 5 min read

Your motorcycle's shock absorbers (or "absorbers" as we commonly call them in Malaysia) are critical components that affect your ride comfort, handling, and most importantly, your safety. Over time, they wear out — and ignoring the signs can lead to dangerous riding conditions.

1. Excessive Bouncing After Speed Bumps

The most obvious sign of worn absorbers is when your motorcycle continues to bounce excessively after hitting a speed bump or pothole. Healthy shock absorbers should dampen the bounce within one or two oscillations. If your bike keeps bouncing like a pogo stick, it's time for a replacement.

2. Oil Leaking from the Absorber Body

Inspect your shock absorbers regularly for oil leaks. If you notice a wet, oily residue around the body or shaft of the absorber, the internal seals have failed. This means the hydraulic fluid is escaping, and the absorber can no longer function properly. This is a clear sign you need new ones immediately.

3. Uneven Tyre Wear

Worn absorbers cause your tyres to make uneven contact with the road surface. Look for patches of uneven wear — especially cupping or scalloping patterns. If you notice this, have your suspension checked as soon as possible. Not only does it indicate absorber problems, but it also means your tyres won't last as long.

4. Nose Diving When Braking

When you apply the brakes and the front of your motorcycle dips dramatically, your front fork absorbers are likely worn. This reduces braking efficiency and can be dangerous in emergency stopping situations. The rear squatting excessively under acceleration is the equivalent sign for rear absorbers.

5. Poor Handling in Corners

If your motorcycle feels unstable, wobbly, or "floaty" when cornering, worn absorbers are often the culprit. The suspension can no longer maintain proper tyre contact with the road, reducing grip and confidence. This is especially dangerous on wet Malaysian roads during monsoon season.

"A quality set of absorbers isn't just about comfort — it's about keeping you safe on the road. Don't compromise on suspension components."

When to Replace?

As a general rule, motorcycle absorbers should be inspected every 20,000 km and replaced every 40,000-50,000 km, depending on riding conditions. However, if you frequently ride on rough Malaysian roads or carry heavy loads, you may need to replace them sooner.
